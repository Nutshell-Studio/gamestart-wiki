    Store Guide

    Your store is one big room with distinct sections. Learning the layout is the game — the better you know it, the faster every box finds its home.

    Finding your way

    Three ways to get your bearings, all showing the same map:

    • Store map — hold Tab any time to raise a paper map of both floors. The red diamond is you; its notch points the way you're facing.
    • Store directory — the reading stand just inside the entrance, to your right as you walk in.
    • Wall map — mounted on the pier beside the front door.

    The map colors match the marquee signs: each platform district fills in its band color, other sections in gray, the counter in red. Ramps are marked with arrows on both floor panels, and every color in the legend is paired with its printed name — you never have to rely on color alone.

    Sections

    The store is now the two-floor layout: a big ground floor plus a mezzanine deck reached by two ramps — half the platform shelving lives upstairs.

    • Platform districts — every platform family owns a grouped district of wall bays: PayStation (blue) along the west, Newtondo (red) along the south, Vapor (black — the PC platform) in the north-west, and XRocks (green) in the north-east and east. Each district continues onto the mezzanine above it; the box shape gives every platform away (chunky cartridge boxes, slim jewel cases, tall keepcases, PC big boxes, little disc cases…). Colored marquee signs overhead mark each district.
    • Free shelves — the island gondolas in the middle of the floor (and a few on the deck): overflow space where any game box can live while you sort.
    • The counter — the heart of the store, under the north mezzanine strip: the register area and the terminal, a CRT computer where you can look up any game in the store and (if you connected Steam) your own play history.
    • Hardware & gear — the console-accessory wall on the east side stocks the console boxes (every platform and generation) alongside the peripherals; these keep their own homes off the game shelves.

    Reading a game box

    The box itself tells you a lot:

    • Front: cover art, platform styling, title.
    • Back: publisher blurb, screenshots, and feature bullets — and, if you connected Steam, a shelf label with your logged hours and the year you last booted the game. Hold V to inspect a box up close; release V to lower it smoothly back into your hand.
    • Planned: the exact numbers move off the box into the terminal, replaced by a fuzzy staff-pick badge that only hints at playtime — never exact numbers.

    Section signs & plaques

    Each section has an overhead sign. Planned: when a section is completely tidied, its sign lights up and stays lit so the store itself becomes your progress bar. The grand opening already waits for every physical copy to be home and the required quiz to be solved.

    Genre dividers (dev-flag preview)

    With the shelf-composer dev flag enabled, the shelves show which genre they want in the style of record-store genre dividers: a colored rail runs along the shelf edge for the whole stretch that wants one genre, with a raised divider tab carrying the genre's name (ACTION, RPG, STRATEGY…) at the start of the stretch and a small matching tab closing its end. Neighboring slots on a shelf row that want the same genre share one divider group — each row of an Action bay on the PayStation wall reads as one named stretch, so every shelf row carries its own name tab along with its colored rail. Shelves open to anything wear a neutral gray divider tab reading Discount or Sale. Each genre keeps its own distinct color everywhere in the store, and the name is always printed on the tab, so you never have to rely on color alone. The dividers are guidance only: they never block items, and they only appear on boots where the composer's plan is active.

    Quiz walls

    Special fixtures that quiz you on your own library:

    • Wall of Shame — the red challenge board: five answer slots under gold rank numbers, reading LEAST PLAYED → MORE PLAYED. Make a game active in your hand, aim at a slot, and interact to set that answer. A display copy appears on the board, but the real copy stays in your hand. Interact with an empty hand to clear an answer. Ties share a rank (a red bracket joins the tied slots). When you're confident, press the red CHECK THE ORDER plate. The five answers glow gold together when the order is right, or red together when it is wrong; changing an answer clears that result.
    • Hall of Honor (Planned) — your most-played games, ranked.

    Getting the Wall of Shame right earns talent points (Talents & Gear) and is required before the grand opening. Price tags never appear on quiz fixtures — that would leak the answers. If you haven't connected Steam, the quiz uses the fictional catalog instead. Answer-setting quizzes are solo-only for now; co-op support is planned.
