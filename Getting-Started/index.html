    Getting Started

    Controls (keyboard & mouse)

    Every control can be rebound in the settings — this is the default layout.

    Action Default
    Move W A S D
    Look Mouse
    Pick up / interact E or Left Click
    Launch an eligible game from its INFONET details page Click Launch or press L
    Put down / throw Right Click (hold to throw harder)
    Drop Q
    Cycle through what you're holding Scroll wheel, or tap R
    Inspect the held box (look closer) Hold F
    Open / close the game's case while inspecting Left Click (while holding F)
    Zoom in for a closer look Hold C
    Check the store map Hold Tab
    Ping a spot for your co-op crew (aim at a player to ask "what/where?") Middle Click
    Open the emote wheel — pick an expression for your co-op crew Hold ` (backtick)
    Use your gear (see Talents & Gear) G
    Cast a skill (see Talents & Gear) 1 2 3 4 5 6
    Jump Space / B (tap or hold when charged jumping is enabled)
    Sprint (hold to run) Left Shift / L3
    Crouch Left Ctrl
    Pause Esc

    A thrown box goes where you are looking: aim up to lob it over something, aim at the floor to bounce it down in front of you. The longer you hold Right Click, the harder it flies — and the faster it spins flat like a frisbee on the way. A quick click still just tosses it gently at your feet, no spin.

    While you hold a throw, the box in your hands starts to shake, and shakes faster the longer you hold — that rattle is your wind-up gauge, so you can judge the throw without looking away from where you're aiming. It settles the moment you let go, cancel, or pause.

    Where charged jumping is enabled, tap for the shortest jump, release partway for a higher jump, or charge fully and release for the highest jump. If automatic jumping is enabled, reaching full charge jumps immediately. A green bar appears below the crosshair only while the jump is charging.

    Gamepad is supported with equivalent bindings — except the skill keys 1–6, which are keyboard-only for now. While the Portal Pistol skill is waiting for you to aim (after pressing 6), Left Click places the portal and Right Click cancels — your usual pick-up and put-down come back as soon as the pistol is done or cancelled. Settings has a toggle-to-run option for Sprint, so you don't have to hold the key down. The settings' ACCESSIBILITY tab collects the motion comfort options — field of view, head bob strength, and a Reduce camera motion switch that calms the camera in one press. The GRAPHICS tab has resolution, refresh rate, fullscreen, and a frame limit (VSync by default). The GENERAL tab holds your player name, the Language dropdown — Auto (system), English, 日本語, 简体中文, 繁體中文, or 繁體中文（香港） — and privacy. A new language applies to screens as they open, and box art keeps its printed language, the way an import shop's shelves would. GENERAL also has a switch that shows each game's genre beside its name in the bottom-right list of what you're holding — it starts off, and you can turn it on.

    Your first night

    Picking NEW GAME shows a LOADING GAME screen while the night is set up — a counter ticks up as the store fills (like 123 / 500 items loaded). The very first night can take a few minutes while cover art is prepared; later nights are much faster. When it finishes, you're in.

    1. Look around. The store opens in "arranged chaos" — games and merch piled on counters, fixtures, and the floor. Everything loose has a home somewhere.
    2. Pick things up (E / Left Click). You can carry a small stack at once — the panel in the bottom-right names the game "in hand" (next to place) in big type, with your carry count out of the max stack (like 3 / 8) under it, and the rest of your pile queued above it — the line just above the big name is what comes out next. Scroll to flip through the stack and the queue reorders with it. The pile you're carrying drifts with your gaze: look up and it settles low so you can see past it, look down and it rides up out of the way — you keep an edge of it in sight either way, and the panel always tells you exactly what you're holding.
    3. Find the right spot. Hold a box near a shelf and a ghost preview shows where it would land. Once you've learned the Stock Sense skill, matching games glow to help you spot where the rest of the stack lives.
    4. Place it. If the spot is right, you get a satisfying chime and XP. If the box is in the wrong section, the shelf's price tag turns red — see Tidying & Price Tags.
    5. Check the terminal. The CRT computer at the counter is the store's brain: look up any game, see your play stats, and browse store info.

    Saving

    The game autosaves when you pause. Progress you make stays made. When a save exists, the title screen shows CONTINUE; picking NEW GAME starts the night over.

    The title screen's LOAD GAME menu holds three save slots. Picking a slot with a save loads it right away; its card shows your level, how many items you've put away, and when you last played. One slot is always the ACTIVE slot — that's the save CONTINUE, NEW GAME, and autosaves all use. Picking an empty slot makes it active so NEW GAME starts there. Starting a new game in one slot never touches the others.

    The title screen also has a SETTINGS menu — the same settings as the pause menu, including your player name.

    If a save can't be continued, the title screen says why in plain words: it may be damaged, from a previous version of the game, from a newer version (update the game to continue), or it may need store content that isn't available any more — for example, after the store's stock lineup changed in an update. Whatever the reason, the fix is the same button: BACK UP SAVE & START NEW GAME — your old save is backed up, never deleted, before the new night begins. If the title screen says it couldn't check your save right now, trying again is safe.
